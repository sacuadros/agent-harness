# Brand and Graphic Design

Create specific visual communication, not decoration or a style preset. Image generation and Figma are production tools; they do not replace art direction.

## Ownership

Own visual identity, art direction, composition, typography, imagery, iconography, static campaign assets, editorial systems, and production readiness. Use:

- `product-design` for application UI, flows, interaction, accessibility behavior, and responsive product surfaces;
- `seo-copy-senior` for positioning, message, claims, copy, and CRO;
- `performance-marketing-senior` for audience, channel, budget, campaign strategy, attribution, and measurement;
- `imagegen` for raster generation or editing after the visual direction is defined.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the communication job

Identify audience, context, channel, physical or digital size, viewing distance, required message, action, brand constraints, exact text, deliverables, and approval owner. Do not begin from a named style.

### 2. Audit visual evidence

Inspect current brand assets, past pieces, source files, typography, colors, imagery, competitors, cultural context, rights, and production constraints. Separate references from anti-references. Read `references/art-direction.md`.

### 3. Set the direction

Define concept, hierarchy, composition, type roles, color roles, image treatment, and one justified signature. Offer 2–3 directions only when a meaningful strategic choice exists. Recommend one and obtain or confirm approval before production.

### 4. Choose the native medium

Prefer editable vector, layout, or code-native assets for logos, icons, type-heavy graphics, diagrams, and repeatable systems. Use Imagegen for raster photography, illustration, textures, or edits. Use Figma when a shared, editable visual system or composed artifact benefits from it. Do not rasterize editable text merely for visual effect.

### 5. Produce minimally

Create the approved master and only the variants required by named channels or formats. Reuse brand primitives; do not generate a new logo, palette, font system, icon family, or campaign motif for one isolated asset unless explicitly requested.

### 6. Critique and refine

Compare the result to the brief, brand evidence, and nearby outputs. Remove generic decoration, competing signatures, unsupported claims, inconsistent image treatment, and accidental template patterns. Validate at actual size and in context, not only on a large artboard.

### 7. Prepare delivery

Read `references/production-and-rights.md` before final export and `references/accessible-visuals.md` for public, informational, text-bearing, or data-driven assets. Inspect every final output for text accuracy, crop, margins, contrast, resolution, profile, compression, naming, rights, and editability.

Do not call work professional, on-brand, accessible, print-ready, or human-made without the corresponding evidence.
